Frequently asked questions

How many calories do I need to build muscle?
Eat 250–500 kcal above your TDEE (maintenance calories). A 250 kcal surplus (lean bulk) minimises fat gain while supporting muscle growth. A 500 kcal surplus builds muscle slightly faster but adds more fat. Use the Weight Gain Calculator to find your exact target.
How much protein do I need to build muscle?
1.6–2.2g of protein per kg of body weight per day is supported by the most comprehensive meta-analyses. For an 80 kg person: 128–176g protein daily. Distribute across 3–4 meals for optimal muscle protein synthesis throughout the day.
How fast can I build muscle naturally?
Beginners can gain 1–2 kg of muscle per month in the first 6–12 months. Intermediate lifters (1–3 years): 0.5–1 kg per month. Advanced (3+ years): 0.25–0.5 kg per month. Expect slower but more permanent gains over time.
Can I build muscle and lose fat at the same time?
Yes — body recomposition is possible for beginners, people returning after a break, and those with higher body fat (20%+ for men, 30%+ for women). It requires eating at maintenance calories, high protein (2.0g/kg), and consistent resistance training. Progress is slower than a dedicated bulk or cut.
What should I eat before and after training?
Pre-workout (1–2h before): 30–40g protein + complex carbs (oats, rice, potato). Post-workout (within 2h): 30–40g protein + fast carbs to replenish glycogen. Total daily protein and calories matter more than exact timing, but pre/post-workout meals optimise training performance and recovery.
Do I need protein supplements to build muscle?
No — whole food protein sources (chicken, eggs, fish, dairy, legumes) are equally effective. Protein powder (whey, casein) is a convenient supplement when whole food intake is inconvenient, but provides no advantage over food when total daily protein is matched.
How do I know if I'm gaining muscle or fat?
Track: body weight (weekly average), waist measurement, strength progress in key lifts (squat, bench, deadlift). If weight is rising 0.25–0.5 kg/week, strength is increasing, and waist is stable — you're gaining mostly muscle. If waist grows proportionally with weight — reduce surplus.
How long should I bulk before cutting?
Bulk until body fat reaches 15–18% (men) or 25–28% (women), then cut to 10–12% (men) or 20–22% (women). A typical lean bulk lasts 3–6 months, followed by a 2–3 month cut. Avoid going above 20% body fat as higher fat levels reduce insulin sensitivity and increase fat gain during a surplus.
