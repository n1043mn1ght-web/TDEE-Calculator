Lentil Health Benefits

Folate — Richest Plant Source

Lentils provide 179µg of folate per 100g (45% DV) — the richest plant source of folate available. A cup provides 358µg — 90% of the daily requirement. Folate is essential for DNA synthesis, cell division, and prevention of neural tube defects during early pregnancy. For pregnant women and those planning pregnancy, lentils are among the most important dietary folate sources.

Prebiotic Fiber — Gut Health

Lentils' 7.9g of fiber per 100g includes significant resistant starch and oligosaccharides that function as prebiotics. Multiple studies show lentil consumption significantly increases Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus populations — beneficial bacteria associated with improved immunity, reduced inflammation, and better metabolic health. The PREDIMED study found legume consumption associated with a 35% reduction in cardiovascular disease risk.

Iron — Richest Plant Source

Lentils provide 3.3mg of iron per 100g — the highest of any legume and among the richest plant sources overall. While non-haem iron from lentils is less bioavailable than haem iron from meat, pairing with vitamin C-rich foods (tomatoes, peppers, lemon) increases absorption 2-6×. A cup of lentils with a squeeze of lemon provides ~6.6mg of iron with meaningfully improved bioavailability.

Cardiovascular Protection — Clinical Evidence

A 2014 meta-analysis of 26 RCTs found legume consumption significantly reduced LDL cholesterol, total cholesterol, and blood pressure. Lentils' combination of soluble fiber (cholesterol-binding), potassium (blood pressure), folate (homocysteine reduction), and phytochemicals makes them one of the most cardioprotective foods available. Regular lentil consumption is associated with 22% lower cardiovascular disease risk.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories in lentils?
Cooked lentils have 116 calories per 100g. A cup (198g): 230 calories and 17.9g protein.
Are lentils high in protein?
Yes — 9g per 100g cooked, 17.9g per cup. Excellent plant protein source, though limiting in methionine. Combine with grains for a complete protein profile.
Are lentils good for weight loss?
Excellent — low GI (32), high fiber (7.9g/100g), and high protein create strong satiety at only 116 kcal/100g. Research shows lentil consumption significantly reduces body weight and waist circumference.
Are lentils keto?
No — 12.2g net carbs per 100g. Not suitable for ketogenic diets.
Are lentils good for diabetics?
Yes — GI of 32 is one of the lowest of any food. Research shows lentil consumption significantly improves glycemic control in type 2 diabetes.
Do lentils need to be soaked?
Unlike other legumes, lentils do not need soaking. They cook in 20-30 min (red lentils: 15 min). Rinsing is recommended.
