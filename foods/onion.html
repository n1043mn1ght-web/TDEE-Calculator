Onion Health Benefits & Nutrition

Quercetin — The Most Studied Flavonoid

Onions are the richest dietary source of quercetin — a flavonoid with extensively documented anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, antihistamine, and cardiovascular effects. Quercetin inhibits histamine release from mast cells, explaining why onion consumption reduces allergy symptoms. It also inhibits LDL oxidation, reduces platelet aggregation, and lowers blood pressure through multiple mechanisms. Outer layers of onion have the highest quercetin concentration — peeling away multiple layers wastes significant amounts. Red onions generally contain more quercetin than white or yellow varieties.

Prebiotic Power — Gut Health

Onions are one of the richest dietary sources of fructooligosaccharides (FOS) — a type of prebiotic fiber that selectively feeds beneficial Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus bacteria. A 2010 clinical trial found that consuming 8g of FOS daily (achievable with 2-3 medium onions) significantly increased Bifidobacterium counts and improved bowel regularity. The prebiotic effect also increases short-chain fatty acid production, which reduces gut inflammation and lowers colon cancer risk.

Cardiovascular Protection

The combination of quercetin and organosulfur compounds in onions provides powerful cardiovascular protection. A meta-analysis of 7 clinical trials found that onion consumption significantly reduced total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol. The Zutphen Elderly Study found that elderly men with the highest onion intake had a 50% lower rate of fatal coronary heart disease. Onion polyphenols inhibit platelet aggregation, reduce blood viscosity, and improve endothelial function — acting as a mild natural anticoagulant.

Anti-Cancer Properties

Onion's organosulfur compounds — particularly allicin, quercetin, and diallyl sulfide — have demonstrated anti-cancer properties across multiple studies. Population studies consistently show that high allium vegetable (onion, garlic, leek) consumption is associated with reduced risk of stomach and colorectal cancer. A Chinese case-control study found that people eating the most allium vegetables had a 79% lower risk of gastric cancer. The anti-cancer mechanisms include inhibiting carcinogen activation, inducing cancer cell apoptosis, and blocking angiogenesis.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories in an onion?
Onions contain 40 calories per 100g. A medium onion (110g) has 44 calories. A large onion (160g) has 64 calories. A tablespoon of chopped onion has about 4 calories. Use the slider above for any weight.
Why do onions make you cry?
When an onion is cut, it releases syn-propanethial-S-oxide — a volatile sulfur compound that reacts with moisture in your eyes to form sulfuric acid, causing the stinging and tearing. Chilling onions before cutting slows the enzyme reaction. Cutting under running water or with a very sharp knife (which crushes fewer cells) also reduces lacrimation.
Are onions good for gut health?
Yes — onions are one of the best prebiotic foods. They contain fructooligosaccharides (FOS) that selectively feed beneficial Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus bacteria. Regular onion consumption significantly increases beneficial bacteria counts and short-chain fatty acid production.
Is onion keto?
In small amounts. Onions have 7.6g net carbs per 100g — a medium onion has 8.4g net carbs. Cooking releases more sugars, increasing the carb impact. Small amounts as flavouring are generally acceptable on keto; half an onion or more per serving is borderline.
Are red onions healthier than white?
Red onions generally contain more quercetin and anthocyanins (from their colour) than white or yellow onions. Yellow onions are also high in quercetin. White onions have the mildest flavour but least antioxidants. For maximum health benefit, red and yellow onions are preferable.
Why do onions cause digestive issues?
Onions are high in fructans — a type of fermentable carbohydrate (FODMAP) that feeds gut bacteria rapidly, causing gas and bloating in sensitive individuals. People with IBS often react strongly to onions. Cooking reduces fructan content somewhat. Small amounts may be tolerated by those with mild sensitivity.
