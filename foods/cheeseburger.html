Cheeseburger Nutrition Facts

Protein and Micronutrient Profile

A cheeseburger is a meaningful source of complete protein (16.8g/100g), vitamin B12 (58% DV per 100g — highest of any fast food), zinc (32% DV), and haem iron (13% DV with excellent bioavailability). For people needing animal-source micronutrients, a cheeseburger — particularly homemade — provides significant nutritional value alongside its calorie content.

Sodium — The Primary Concern

At 551mg sodium per 100g, a standard cheeseburger provides ~825mg of sodium — 36% of the daily limit. Fast food versions with sauces and pickles reach 1,200-1,500mg per burger. Regular fast food cheeseburger consumption is strongly associated with hypertension due to this sodium load. Homemade versions without added salt can reduce sodium by 50-60%.

Saturated Fat — Know the Amount

A standard cheeseburger contains approximately 5.8g of saturated fat per 100g — 29% of daily recommended maximum. A large fast food double cheeseburger can contain 15-20g of saturated fat — a full day's allowance. Choosing lean beef (90%+ lean) and one rather than two patties significantly reduces saturated fat content.

Fast Food vs Homemade

The calorie and nutrient gap between homemade and fast-food cheeseburgers is substantial. Homemade (150g, lean beef, 1 cheese slice): ~380 kcal, 600mg sodium, 8g sat fat. McDonald's Quarter Pounder with Cheese: 520 kcal, 1,090mg sodium, 13g sat fat. Making burgers at home with quality ingredients significantly improves the nutritional profile.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories in a cheeseburger?
A standard cheeseburger (150g) has ~443 calories. Per 100g: 295 kcal. McDonald's McDouble: 400 kcal. Quarter Pounder with cheese: 520 kcal. Big Mac: 550 kcal.
Is a cheeseburger healthy?
A homemade cheeseburger with lean beef, one slice of cheese, and a whole-grain bun can be a reasonable meal. Fast food versions with double patties, special sauce, and large buns are high in sodium, saturated fat, and refined carbs.
How much protein in a cheeseburger?
A standard 150g cheeseburger provides ~25g of protein — a significant contribution to daily protein needs.
Is a cheeseburger keto?
Without the bun: yes — the beef and cheese patty is ~3g net carbs. With the bun: no — adds ~25g carbs.
How to make a healthier cheeseburger?
Lean beef (90%+ lean), one slice of cheese instead of two, whole-grain bun, load with vegetables (lettuce, tomato, onion), mustard instead of mayo. Skip bacon and special sauces.
Cheeseburger vs hamburger calories?
Hamburger (no cheese, 150g): ~380 kcal. Cheeseburger (150g): ~443 kcal. The cheese slice adds ~60 kcal.
