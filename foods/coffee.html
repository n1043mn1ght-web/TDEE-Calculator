Coffee Health Benefits

Richest Antioxidant Source in the Western Diet

Coffee is the single largest source of antioxidants in the diet of most people in Western countries — exceeding fruits, vegetables, and tea in terms of antioxidant contribution per day. This is due to both the high antioxidant content (chlorogenic acids, caffeic acid, ferulic acid) and high daily consumption volume. A single cup provides more antioxidants than a typical serving of blueberries. These antioxidants reduce oxidative stress associated with ageing, cardiovascular disease, and neurodegeneration.

Type 2 Diabetes — 25% Lower Risk

A 2014 meta-analysis of 28 prospective studies found that each additional cup of coffee per day was associated with a 6% lower risk of type 2 diabetes. Three cups/day corresponds to ~25% lower risk. The mechanisms include: chlorogenic acids reducing intestinal glucose absorption, improving insulin sensitivity, and reducing hepatic glucose production; caffeine improving fat oxidation; and magnesium improving insulin receptor function.

Parkinson's Disease — 32-60% Risk Reduction

Coffee consumption is one of the most consistently observed environmental protective factors against Parkinson's disease. Multiple large studies find 32-60% lower Parkinson's risk in regular coffee drinkers. Caffeine directly blocks adenosine A2A receptors in the brain, which are involved in Parkinson's pathology. This mechanism is so strong that adenosine A2A receptor antagonists are being developed as potential Parkinson's treatments.

Liver Protection — 40% Lower Cirrhosis Risk

Regular coffee consumption is associated with 40% lower risk of liver cirrhosis and 15% lower risk of liver cancer per 2 cups/day. Coffee increases liver enzyme expression (CYP1A2) that processes and excretes toxins. It also reduces liver fibrosis by lowering transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) — the primary driver of liver scarring. Even in people with pre-existing liver disease, coffee consumption significantly slows disease progression.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories in coffee?
Black coffee has 2 calories per 100ml — about 5 calories per 240ml cup. It's essentially calorie-free. However, additions add up fast: cream (+52 kcal/tbsp), sugar (+16 kcal/tsp), milk (+9 kcal/30ml splash).
Is coffee good for you?
Large epidemiological studies consistently show coffee consumption associated with reduced risk of type 2 diabetes (25%), Parkinson's disease (32-60%), Alzheimer's disease (65%), liver cirrhosis (40%), and several cancers. 3-5 cups daily appears optimal.
How much caffeine in a cup of coffee?
Brewed coffee: ~95mg per 8oz cup (240ml). Espresso: ~63mg per shot. Instant coffee: ~60mg. Decaf: 2-7mg. Safe for most adults up to 400mg/day (4 cups).
Does coffee break intermittent fasting?
Black coffee: No — virtually zero calories and may enhance fasting benefits (autophagy, fat burning). Coffee with milk or sugar: Yes — breaks the fast.
Is coffee keto?
Yes — black coffee has zero net carbs. One of the most popular keto beverages. Bulletproof coffee (with butter/MCT oil) is keto-popular but adds 200-400 kcal.
When is the best time to drink coffee?
90-120 minutes after waking allows cortisol to peak naturally first. Caffeine works best when cortisol is declining. Last cup ideally 6 hours before sleep — caffeine has a 5-6 hour half-life.
