Hummus Health Benefits

Glycemic Index ~6 — Extraordinary Blood Sugar Control

With a GI of approximately 6 — one of the lowest of any food tested — hummus produces virtually no blood sugar elevation. The combination of chickpea fiber, resistant starch, tahini fat, and olive oil completely blunts glucose absorption. Adding hummus to a meal with bread or crackers substantially reduces the overall glycemic response of that meal.

Chickpea and Tahini Synergy

Hummus combines nutritionally complementary ingredients: chickpeas (high in lysine, low in methionine) and sesame/tahini (higher in methionine, lower in lysine). The combination creates a more complete amino acid profile than either alone — a traditional example of complementary plant protein combining practiced long before modern nutrition science identified the rationale.

Dietary Quality Association

A 2016 Nutrients study found hummus and chickpea consumers had significantly better overall dietary quality: higher intake of fiber, vitamins, and minerals; lower intake of sugar and saturated fat; 53% lower obesity risk; and 49% lower waist circumference.

Tahini — Hidden Nutrition

Tahini in hummus contributes: calcium (182mg/100g), copper, zinc, and sesamin — a lignan with antioxidant properties. Sesame is also rich in phytosterols that reduce LDL cholesterol absorption. The tahini component provides approximately 30-40% of hummus's total nutritional value.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories in hummus?
Hummus has 177 calories per 100g. 2 tablespoons (30g): 53 kcal. A 120g portion: 212 kcal.
Is hummus healthy?
Yes — one of the most nutritious dips available. Good protein, fiber, iron, and folate from chickpeas; healthy fat from tahini and olive oil; very low GI (~6). Studies show regular hummus consumers have better dietary quality and lower BMI.
Is hummus good for weight loss?
Yes in moderate portions. Very low GI, fiber, and protein create excellent satiety per calorie. 2-4 tbsp with vegetables is ideal.
Is hummus keto?
In small portions — 17.2g carbs per 100g. 2 tablespoons (30g) has 5g net carbs — manageable in small amounts.
Homemade vs commercial hummus?
Homemade: ~170 kcal, ~100mg sodium. Commercial: 177 kcal, ~379mg sodium. Making at home reduces sodium by 75%.
What to dip in hummus?
Best: raw vegetables (carrots, celery, cucumber — ~10-20 kcal each), wholemeal pitta (~80 kcal/piece), rice cakes (35 kcal). Avoid: chips and crackers (100-150 kcal per handful).
