Quick summary: Pears contain 57 calories per 100g and 101 calories per medium fruit. They have one of the lowest glycemic indices of any fruit (~38) because their sugars are predominantly fructose. A medium pear provides 22% of daily fiber requirement (mostly pectin in the skin) and significant quercetin and catechin antioxidants. Clinical trials show daily pear consumption reduces blood pressure and LDL cholesterol meaningfully.

Pear Health Benefits & Nutrition

Highest Fructose-to-Glucose Ratio — Why Pears Have a Low GI

Pears are unique among common fruits in having an exceptionally high ratio of fructose to glucose. Unlike glucose, fructose does not trigger an insulin response directly — it is metabolised primarily in the liver. This is why pears have one of the lowest glycemic indices of any fruit (~38), despite containing 9.75g of sugar per 100g. This makes pears an excellent fruit choice for people managing blood sugar.

Fiber — Mostly in the Skin

Pears contain 3.1g of fiber per 100g, and a significant proportion — particularly pectin (soluble fiber) and lignin (insoluble fiber) — is concentrated in the skin. Eating a pear with its skin provides roughly 50% more fiber than a peeled pear. A medium pear with skin provides 22% of the daily fiber requirement.

Cardiovascular Benefits

A 2021 randomised controlled trial found that adults who ate two pears daily for 12 weeks had significant reductions in blood pressure (3.5 mmHg systolic) and LDL cholesterol compared to controls. These effects were attributed to pear fiber (which binds bile acids and reduces cholesterol recirculation) and pear polyphenols including quercetin, catechin, and epicatechin.

Boron and Bone Health

Pears are a good dietary source of boron — a trace mineral that plays important roles in bone health, hormone metabolism, and cognitive function. Boron reduces calcium and magnesium excretion through urine, effectively increasing the body's retention of these bone-building minerals. Adequate boron intake is associated with stronger bones and may reduce the risk of osteoarthritis.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories in a pear?
Pears contain 57 calories per 100g. A medium pear (178g) has 101 calories. A small pear (90g) has 51 calories. Use the slider above to calculate for any weight.
Should I eat the pear skin?
Yes — pear skin contains a significant portion of the fruit's fiber and antioxidants. Eating a pear with skin provides about 50% more fiber than a peeled pear, plus additional quercetin and polyphenols concentrated in the skin.
Is pear good for weight loss?
Yes. Pears provide 57 kcal per 100g with 3.1g of fiber and 84% water content. Research shows pear fiber (particularly pectin) slows gastric emptying and increases satiety hormones, making pears effective for appetite control.
Is pear good for diabetics?
Yes — pears are one of the best fruits for diabetics with a GI of approximately 38 (one of the lowest of any fruit). Their sugars are mostly fructose, which doesn't trigger insulin directly. Their fiber further slows sugar absorption.
Is pear keto?
No. Pears have 12.1g of net carbs per 100g. A medium pear (178g) provides 21.6g of net carbs — far too many for a ketogenic diet.
What is the difference between pear varieties?
Common varieties include Bartlett/Williams (yellow-green, juicy, sweet), Bosc (brown, firm, nutty), Anjou (green or red, mild), and Conference (green, elongated). Calorically all varieties are similar (~57 kcal/100g). Bartlett/Williams are the sweetest; Bosc the firmest.
