Peanut Butter Benefits

Satiety — Reduces Calorie Intake

A 2013 Nutrition & Metabolism study found peanut butter increased satiety for up to 12 hours, with participants consuming fewer calories throughout the day. The protein (25g/100g), fat (50g/100g), and fiber (6g/100g) activate multiple satiety pathways. Regular consumption is consistently associated with better weight management despite caloric density.

Niacin — Cardiovascular Protection

10.7mg niacin per 100g (67% DV). Harvard Nurses' Health Study found 5+ servings of nuts/peanut butter per week was associated with 44% lower risk of type 2 diabetes in women. Niacin provides essential NAD for energy metabolism and DNA repair.

Heart Health Evidence

Multiple large prospective studies show peanut butter reduces cardiovascular disease risk. PREDIMED trial found nut-supplemented Mediterranean diets reduced cardiovascular events by 30%. Unsaturated fat, vitamin E, niacin, and resveratrol all contribute.

Vitamin E and Magnesium

Two tablespoons provide 19% of daily vitamin E and 12% of daily magnesium — two of the most commonly deficient nutrients in Western diets. Peanut butter is one of the most practical sources for improving intake of both simultaneously.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories in peanut butter?
Peanut butter has 588 calories per 100g. 2 tablespoons (32g): 188 kcal and 8g protein. 1 tablespoon (16g): 94 kcal.
Is peanut butter healthy?
Yes in moderate amounts — rich in niacin, vitamin E, magnesium, and protein. Regular consumption is associated with reduced cardiovascular risk. Choose natural (peanuts only) to avoid added sugar.
How much peanut butter per day?
2 tablespoons (32g, 188 kcal) is the standard serving used in research. Provides 8g protein, 2g fiber, and meaningful niacin, vitamin E, and magnesium.
Natural vs commercial peanut butter?
Natural: no added sugar, no palm oil, ~10mg sodium. Commercial: 1-3g added sugar, palm oil, ~432mg sodium. Natural is nutritionally superior.
Is peanut butter keto?
Yes — 14.1g net carbs per 100g. Two tablespoons have 4.5g net carbs.
Does peanut butter cause weight gain?
Research shows it does not cause expected weight gain. Its protein-fat-fiber combination is highly satiating, reducing total calorie intake.
