Potato Health Benefits & Nutrition

Potassium — More Than a Banana

Potatoes are one of the richest dietary sources of potassium, providing 425mg per 100g compared to 358mg in bananas. A medium baked potato (with skin) provides nearly 900mg of potassium — 26% of the daily requirement. Potassium is essential for blood pressure regulation, muscle function, and heart health. Research consistently shows high-potassium diets reduce cardiovascular disease risk. The potassium in potatoes is highly bioavailable — particularly in baked or boiled preparations.

Resistant Starch — The Gut Health Secret

When cooked potatoes are cooled, some of their starch converts to resistant starch — a type of fiber that feeds beneficial gut bacteria. Cooled potato salad has significantly more resistant starch than freshly cooked potato. Resistant starch improves insulin sensitivity, increases satiety hormones, and selectively feeds Bifidobacterium and other beneficial gut bacteria. Reheating once-cooled potatoes only partially reverses this conversion.

Satiety — Most Filling Food Studied

A 1995 satiety index study found potatoes to be the most satiating food tested — 323% as filling as white bread by calorie. This makes them excellent for weight management despite their carb content. The combination of water content, fiber, protein, and resistant starch creates strong satiety signals. Evidence suggests people eat fewer calories in subsequent meals after consuming potatoes.

Vitamin C and Antioxidants

A medium potato provides 28mg of vitamin C — 31% of the daily requirement. Potatoes also contain chlorogenic acid (the main antioxidant in coffee), quercetin, and patatin — a protein with antioxidant activity unique to potatoes. Purple and blue potatoes contain anthocyanins with additional antioxidant effects. The antioxidant content is concentrated near the skin — eating potatoes with skin preserves significantly more nutrients.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories in a potato?
Potatoes contain 77 calories per 100g raw. A medium potato (213g) has 164 calories. Boiled potato has ~87 kcal/100g, baked with skin ~93 kcal/100g, and french fries ~312 kcal/100g. Use the slider above for any weight.
Are potatoes fattening?
Potatoes themselves are not fattening — 77 kcal per 100g is moderate for a filling food. Research suggests plain potatoes are among the most satiating foods studied. What makes potatoes high-calorie is cooking method: frying and adding butter, sour cream, or cheese dramatically increases calories.
Are potatoes good for you?
Yes. Potatoes are rich in potassium (more than bananas), vitamin C, vitamin B6, and resistant starch. They are naturally fat-free and filling. Baked or boiled potatoes with skin are a nutritious whole food — it is the processing (frying, chips) that makes them unhealthy.
Is potato keto?
No. Potatoes have 15.4g of net carbs per 100g — a medium potato has 34g of net carbs, far exceeding daily keto limits (20-50g total). Even a small portion would use most of a day's carb allowance on keto.
What is a nutritious way to eat potatoes?
Boiling or baking with skin preserves the most nutrients. Cooling after cooking converts starch to beneficial resistant starch. Avoid frying and minimise high-calorie toppings. Eating potatoes with the skin significantly increases fiber, potassium, and antioxidant content.
Do potatoes have more potassium than bananas?
Yes — potatoes contain 425mg of potassium per 100g, compared to 358mg in bananas. A medium baked potato provides nearly 900mg of potassium, making it one of the best dietary sources of this essential mineral.
