Tomato Health Benefits & Nutrition

Lycopene — Cancer-Fighting Antioxidant

Tomatoes are the richest dietary source of lycopene — a carotenoid with powerful antioxidant properties. A landmark Harvard study of 47,000 men found that eating tomato-based foods more than twice weekly reduced prostate cancer risk by 35%. Crucially, lycopene is significantly more bioavailable from cooked tomatoes than raw — heating breaks down cell walls and concentrates lycopene, while fat further increases absorption. Tomato sauce, paste, and sun-dried tomatoes have 3–5× more lycopene than fresh tomatoes.

Heart Health

Tomatoes are strongly associated with cardiovascular protection. Lycopene reduces LDL oxidation — the key step in arterial plaque formation. A meta-analysis of 21 studies found that high tomato intake was associated with 14% lower cardiovascular disease risk. The combination of lycopene, potassium, vitamin C, and flavonoids (quercetin, kaempferol) provides multi-pathway cardiovascular protection.

Vitamin C and Skin Health

A medium tomato provides 17mg of vitamin C — 19% of the daily requirement. Vitamin C is essential for collagen synthesis and photoprotection. Tomatoes also contain beta-carotene and lycopene, both of which provide UV photoprotection when consumed regularly. Evidence suggests high tomato consumption measurably reduces UV-induced skin reddening.

Blood Sugar Friendly

With only 18 kcal per 100g and 2.7g of net carbs, tomatoes have an extremely low glycemic index (~15). Chromium in tomatoes helps regulate blood sugar, and naringenin — a flavonoid found in tomatoes — has shown anti-diabetic properties in research. Tomatoes are one of the few foods freely consumed on virtually all therapeutic diets including keto, diabetic, and weight loss protocols.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories in a tomato?
Tomatoes contain 18 calories per 100g. A medium tomato (123g) has 22 calories. A large tomato (182g) has 33 calories. A cup of chopped tomatoes (180g) has 32 calories. Use the slider above for any weight.
Is tomato a fruit or vegetable?
Botanically, tomato is a fruit (it develops from a flower and contains seeds). Culinarily and legally, it is classified as a vegetable in most countries. The US Supreme Court ruled in 1893 that tomatoes are vegetables for trade purposes. For nutrition purposes, it makes no difference.
Are cooked tomatoes healthier than raw?
For lycopene — yes. Cooking breaks down cell walls, making lycopene 2–3× more bioavailable. Eating tomatoes with fat (olive oil) further increases lycopene absorption by 4×. For vitamin C — raw is better, as heat degrades some vitamin C. Overall, both raw and cooked tomatoes are excellent; variety is ideal.
Is tomato keto?
Yes. Tomatoes have only 2.7g of net carbs per 100g and 18 kcal. A medium tomato has just 3.3g of net carbs, making them compatible with ketogenic diets in normal serving sizes.
How much lycopene is in a tomato?
A medium raw tomato contains approximately 3,165µg of lycopene. Cooked tomato products are much higher: tomato paste has ~42,000µg/100g, tomato sauce ~15,000µg/100g. Eating tomato-based cooked foods provides substantially more bioavailable lycopene than fresh tomatoes.
Do tomatoes lower blood pressure?
Evidence suggests yes. A 2006 study found that tomato extract supplementation reduced systolic blood pressure by 10 mmHg in hypertensive patients. The effect is attributed to lycopene reducing oxidative stress in blood vessel walls, combined with potassium's natriuretic effect. Regular tomato consumption is associated with lower blood pressure in population studies.
