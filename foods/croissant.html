Croissant Nutrition Facts

The Laminating Technique — Why So Much Butter

A traditional French croissant incorporates 250-300g of butter per kilogram of dough — 25-30% of the finished product by weight. This lamination technique is responsible for both the flaky texture and the high calorie and saturated fat content.

Protein — A Relative Bright Spot

At 8.2g protein per 100g, a croissant has more protein than most comparable pastries (muffin: 5.9g, donut: 5.7g) due to egg content. Pairing with eggs or smoked salmon significantly improves the protein content and satiety of the meal.

Glycemic Response — GI 67

Despite containing butter, a croissant's GI of approximately 67 is relatively high — driven by refined white flour. The blood sugar response is significantly higher than sourdough bread (GI ~52) or whole-grain bread (GI ~49). Whole-grain versions reduce GI and add fiber.

Plain vs Filled — Calorie Gap

Plain butter croissant (57g): 231 kcal. Almond croissant (same size, filled with almond cream): ~360 kcal. Ham and cheese croissant (120g): ~456 kcal. Plain is always the lowest-calorie choice.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories in a croissant?
A croissant has 406 calories per 100g. Standard (57g): 231 kcal. Large bakery (80g): ~325 kcal. Almond croissant (100g): ~450 kcal.
Why are croissants so high in calories?
Croissants use the laminating technique — folding and rolling butter into dough repeatedly creating 27-81 layers of dough and butter. This incorporates 25-30% of the dough weight as butter, accounting for most of the calorie content.
Are croissants healthy?
Not nutritionally valuable — high in refined flour, saturated fat (12g/100g), and sodium with minimal vitamins, minerals, or fiber. As an occasional breakfast treat, fine. As a daily choice, concerning.
Croissant vs bagel?
Bagel (98g): 270 kcal, 1.5g fat, 54g carbs. Croissant (57g): 231 kcal, 12g fat, 26g carbs. Per gram: croissant is more calorie-dense. Per serving, similar total calories.
Is a croissant keto?
No — 45.8g carbs per 100g.
How to make a healthier choice?
Choose plain over filled, smaller over large, pair with protein (eggs, smoked salmon), coffee without added sugar.
