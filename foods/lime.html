Lime Health Benefits & Nutrition

Limonoids — Unique Anti-Cancer Compounds

Limes (and all citrus) contain limonoids — a class of terpene compounds responsible for the bitter taste in citrus juice. Limonin, the primary limonoid in lime, has demonstrated anti-cancer properties in cell and animal studies, inhibiting the growth of breast, colon, stomach, lung, and mouth cancer cells. Limonoids are water-soluble and remain active in the body for 24 hours after consumption — longer than most antioxidants.

Scurvy Prevention — Historical Significance

Lime's historical importance in nutrition is extraordinary. British sailors in the 18th and 19th centuries were given lime juice to prevent scurvy (severe vitamin C deficiency) on long voyages — giving rise to the nickname 'Limeys'. This was one of the first recorded controlled clinical nutrition experiments, predating modern clinical trials by over a century.

Kidney Stone Prevention

Like lemons, limes are high in citric acid which binds to urinary calcium and inhibits calcium oxalate crystal formation. Lime juice consumption consistently raises urinary citrate levels — the key mechanism for preventing kidney stones. A cup of fresh lime juice provides approximately 1.1g of citric acid, which is the therapeutic dose used in many kidney stone prevention protocols.

Iron Absorption Enhancement

Like all citrus, lime juice dramatically increases absorption of non-haem iron (plant-based iron) when consumed together. A squeeze of lime juice over iron-rich plant foods like lentils, beans, or leafy greens can increase iron absorption by 2–4 times — particularly important for vegetarians and vegans.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories in a lime?
Limes contain 30 calories per 100g. A medium lime (67g of flesh) has about 20 calories. A tablespoon of lime juice (15ml) has only 4 calories. Use the slider above to calculate for any weight.
What is the difference between lime and lemon?
Limes are smaller, greener, more tart, and lower in sugar (1.7g vs 2.5g per 100g). Lemons have more vitamin C (53mg vs 29mg per 100g). Both have similar citric acid content and kidney stone prevention properties. Limes contain more limonoids; lemons contain more hesperidin.
Is lime juice keto?
Yes. A tablespoon of lime juice contains only about 1.4g of carbs and 1.1g of net carbs — negligible for keto. Whole lime flesh has 7.7g of net carbs per 100g, but portions used in cooking are typically small enough to be keto-compatible.
Does lime help with digestion?
Yes. The citric acid in lime stimulates gastric acid production, which may improve protein digestion. Lime juice before meals is a traditional digestive aid in many cultures.
Is lime good for skin?
Yes. Lime's vitamin C supports collagen synthesis. However, lime juice applied directly to skin can cause phototoxic reactions (lime burns) when exposed to sunlight. Always rinse lime juice from skin before sun exposure.
Can you substitute lime for lemon?
In most recipes, yes — lime can replace lemon in a 1:1 ratio. The flavour profile differs: lime is more floral and tropical, lemon more classic and bright. Both provide similar acidity and vitamin C content.
