Cauliflower Health Benefits & Nutrition

Choline — The Underrated Nutrient

Cauliflower is one of the few plant foods with meaningful choline content — 44.3mg per 100g. Choline is essential for brain development, liver function, nerve signal transmission, and cell membrane integrity. Most adults are deficient in choline (only 8% of Americans meet the adequate intake). Choline deficiency is associated with fatty liver disease, neurological impairment, and impaired fetal brain development. As a plant-based choline source, cauliflower is particularly valuable for vegans who cannot rely on eggs and meat.

Low-Carb Culinary Versatility

Cauliflower's mild flavour and texture make it the most versatile low-carb substitute in cooking. Cauliflower rice (grated and sautéed) provides a grain-free alternative with 3g net carbs vs 28g in white rice. Mashed cauliflower replaces mashed potato with 70% fewer calories. Cauliflower pizza crust reduces carbs by 60% vs flour crust. These substitutions have driven enormous cauliflower popularity in low-carb and keto communities.

Vitamin C — More Than an Orange

Cauliflower provides 48.2mg of vitamin C per 100g — 54% of the daily requirement, and slightly more than a medium orange (53mg per 100g). The vitamin C in cauliflower supports immune function, collagen synthesis, and iron absorption. Because cauliflower is not typically thought of as a vitamin C source, this is frequently overlooked.

Antioxidant support — Glucosinolates and Sulforaphane

Like broccoli, cauliflower contains glucosinolates that convert to sulforaphane and other isothiocyanates when chewed. These compounds activate supports natural cleansing enzymes (phase II enzymes) that neutralise carcinogens before they can damage DNA. Population studies consistently show that high cruciferous vegetable consumption is associated with reduced risk of lung, colorectal, breast, and prostate cancer. Cauliflower provides a meaningful dose of these protective compounds in a low-calorie package.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories in cauliflower?
Cauliflower contains 25 calories per 100g. A cup of cauliflower florets (107g) has 27 calories. A medium head (160g) has 40 calories. Use the slider above for any weight.
Is cauliflower good for keto?
Yes — cauliflower is the best keto-friendly vegetable for cooking substitutes. With only 3.0g of net carbs per 100g, it can replace rice (28g net carbs), mashed potato (15g net carbs), and pizza crust. A cup of cauliflower rice has 3.2g net carbs vs 36g in a cup of cooked white rice.
Is cauliflower or broccoli more nutritious?
Broccoli is generally more nutritious: it has more vitamin C (89mg vs 48mg), more vitamin K (102µg vs 16µg), more protein (2.82g vs 1.92g), more sulforaphane, and more fibre. Cauliflower has more choline and fewer calories. Both are excellent — eating a variety of cruciferous vegetables is optimal.
Why does cauliflower cause gas?
Cauliflower contains raffinose (a trisaccharide) and glucosinolates that are fermented by gut bacteria, producing gas. This is more pronounced in people with fewer Bacteroides bacteria (which efficiently digest these compounds). Cooking reduces the gas-causing compounds. Introducing cruciferous vegetables gradually allows gut bacteria to adapt.
Can you eat cauliflower raw?
Yes — raw cauliflower retains more glucosinolates and vitamin C than cooked. It can be eaten as crudités with dip, grated into salads, or used in raw preparations. Raw cauliflower has a firmer texture and slightly bitter, peppery flavour from glucosinolates. Cutting it 40+ minutes before eating allows myrosinase to convert more glucosinolates to beneficial compounds.
How many calories does cauliflower rice save compared to regular rice?
A cup of cooked white rice (186g) has approximately 242 calories and 53g of carbs. A cup of cauliflower rice (107g) has 27 calories and 3.2g net carbs. That is a saving of 215 calories and 50g of carbs per serving — very significant for weight loss or carb reduction.
