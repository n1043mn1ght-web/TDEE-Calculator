Cabbage Health Benefits & Nutrition

Gut Health — L-Glutamine and Fermentation

Raw cabbage juice has been used to treat gastric ulcers since the 1950s — a clinical trial found it healed peptic ulcers in an average of 10 days vs 37 days for standard treatment. The active compound is L-glutamine — an amino acid that nourishes the gut lining cells (enterocytes). Fermented cabbage (sauerkraut, kimchi) adds probiotic bacteria, significantly enhancing gut health benefits. A 2021 study found that fermented food consumption (including sauerkraut) increased microbiome diversity and reduced inflammatory markers more effectively than a high-fiber diet.

Sulforaphane and Cancer Prevention

Like broccoli and cauliflower, cabbage is a cruciferous vegetable rich in glucosinolates that convert to sulforaphane when chewed. Sulforaphane activates NRF2 — the master regulator of antioxidant defence — and has demonstrated anti-cancer properties across dozens of studies. Population studies consistently show high cruciferous vegetable intake is associated with reduced risk of multiple cancers. Cabbage is a more affordable and accessible cruciferous vegetable than broccoli for regular consumption.

Vitamin K for Bone and Cardiovascular Health

Cabbage provides 76µg of vitamin K per 100g — 63% of the daily requirement. Vitamin K1 activates osteocalcin (bone-building) and matrix Gla-protein (may help reduce risk of arterial calcification). Low vitamin K is independently associated with osteoporosis and cardiovascular events. Red cabbage provides additional vitamin K and is one of the most nutritionally comprehensive low-calorie foods available.

Anti-Inflammatory Anthocyanins (Red Cabbage)

Red cabbage contains anthocyanins — the purple/red pigments with powerful anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties. Evidence suggests anthocyanins reduce LDL oxidation, lower blood pressure, and inhibit inflammatory markers (TNF-α, IL-6). Red cabbage has up to 36 different types of anthocyanins. Cooking reduces anthocyanin content, so raw red cabbage (salads, coleslaw) preserves the most benefit.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories in cabbage?
Cabbage contains 25 calories per 100g. A cup of shredded raw cabbage (89g) has 22 calories. A cup of cooked cabbage (150g) has 34 calories. Use the slider above for any weight.
Is cabbage good for gut health?
Yes — raw cabbage juice has clinical evidence for healing gastric ulcers. L-glutamine in cabbage nourishes the gut lining. Fermented cabbage (sauerkraut, kimchi) provides probiotics that increase microbiome diversity and reduce inflammation.
Is red or green cabbage healthier?
Red cabbage is significantly more nutritious: it contains more vitamin C, more vitamin K, more antioxidants, and anthocyanins (from the purple colour) which green cabbage lacks. Both are healthy, but red cabbage delivers more nutritional value per calorie.
Is cabbage keto?
Yes — cabbage has only 3.3g of net carbs per 100g. A cup of shredded cabbage has just 2.9g of net carbs, making it one of the best keto-friendly vegetables for volume and crunch.
Is sauerkraut healthier than raw cabbage?
Different benefits. Raw cabbage has more vitamin C (fermentation reduces it). Sauerkraut has beneficial probiotics (Lactobacillus species), higher bioavailability of some nutrients, and potential gut health benefits from fermentation. Both are healthy — ideally eat both regularly.
Does cabbage cause gas?
In some people, yes. Cabbage contains raffinose and glucosinolates fermented by gut bacteria, producing gas. Cooking reduces this effect. People with IBS may be more sensitive. Starting with small amounts and gradually increasing helps the gut microbiome adapt.
