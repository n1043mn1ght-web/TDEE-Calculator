Salmon Health Benefits

Omega-3 — The Most Important Nutrient in Salmon

Salmon provides 2.2g of EPA and DHA omega-3 fatty acids per 100g — the richest common dietary source. EPA and DHA are essential for brain function, reduce triglycerides by 15-30%, associated with cardiovascular health in research by 2-5 mmHg, and reduce cardiovascular events by 10-15% according to meta-analyses. Unlike plant omega-3 (ALA), EPA and DHA are directly bioavailable without conversion. A single salmon fillet provides the entire weekly omega-3 recommendation from most health authorities.

Vitamin D — Critical Nutrient, Rare Food Source

Salmon provides 13.1µg of vitamin D per 100g — 127% of the daily value. Vitamin D deficiency affects over 1 billion people worldwide and is associated with bone disease, immune dysfunction, depression, and increased cancer risk. Very few foods naturally contain meaningful vitamin D. A 100g portion of salmon is one of the most effective dietary interventions for vitamin D status, particularly in regions with limited sun exposure.

Brain Health and Cognitive Protection

DHA (the omega-3 in salmon) is the primary structural fat in the human brain — comprising 40% of brain polyunsaturated fatty acids. Regular fatty fish consumption is associated with 47% lower risk of Alzheimer's disease in prospective studies. The MIDAS trial found that 900mg DHA daily improved learning and memory in older adults with age-related cognitive decline. Salmon's combination of DHA, B12, selenium, and antioxidants makes it uniquely protective for brain health.

Astaxanthin — Unique Antioxidant

Salmon gets its pink-red colour from astaxanthin — a carotenoid with antioxidant potency 6,000× stronger than vitamin C. Astaxanthin reduces oxidative stress in muscles (reducing post-exercise soreness), protects skin from UV damage, reduces inflammation, and crosses the blood-brain barrier. Unlike most carotenoids, astaxanthin is not converted to vitamin A in the body and accumulates in mitochondria where it protects cellular energy production.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories in salmon?
Salmon has 208 calories per 100g. A fillet (170g): 354 kcal and 35g protein. Wild salmon is leaner: ~140-170 kcal/100g.
Is salmon high in omega-3?
Yes — salmon provides 2.2g of EPA+DHA per 100g — the highest of any common fish. A fillet covers the weekly omega-3 recommendation in one meal.
Is salmon good for weight loss?
Yes. Despite being relatively calorie-dense, salmon's high protein (20g/100g) and omega-3s improve satiety and support fat metabolism. Evidence suggests omega-3 supplementation improves body composition.
How often should you eat salmon?
The American Heart Association recommends 2 servings of fatty fish per week. This provides adequate omega-3 for may support cardiovascular health.
Wild vs farmed salmon calories?
Wild salmon: ~140-170 kcal/100g, ~6-9g fat. Farmed: ~208 kcal, ~13g fat. Both have similar omega-3 content. Wild is leaner; farmed is more available and affordable.
Is salmon keto?
Yes — zero carbs, high healthy fat and protein. Salmon is one of the best keto foods.
